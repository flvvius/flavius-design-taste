# Voice

Specific work, interests and places beat promotional claims. This wording is inherited from Personal room. The shared rule below is an inference from the portfolio's first-person writing and Pricele's task-based explanations. A client's established voice still takes precedence.

| Rule and reason | Use it here | Limit | Example | Anti-example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sentence case keeps functional labels readable. | App headings and controls. | Portfolio lowercase and meaningful Pricele standing heads remain legitimate. | Account details | ACCOUNT DETAILS on every row |
| Name the result with a verb. Readers should predict the action. | Buttons, menus and links. | A personal invitation can suit a clear destination. | Save preferences | Unlock your potential |
| State what is empty and give a useful next step. | Empty collections and search results. | Do not offer a create action when permissions prohibit it. | No projects yet. Create a project. | A smiling mascot and Nothing to see here |
| Describe a failure and recovery plainly. | Errors and validation. | Do not invent a cause or promise data was saved. | We couldn't save this draft. Try again. | Oops, something magical went wrong |
| State the actual loading activity. | Waiting for work or data. | A brief local update may need no status sentence. | Loading invoices | Hang tight, greatness is coming |
| Keep facts and the owner's wording. Specificity makes a page recognizable. | About, service and project copy. | Private or unverified claims never become public proof. | Full-stack engineer in Bucharest | Crafting transformative digital experiences |
| Explain the consequence of destructive actions. | Delete, revoke and permission changes. | Preserve the existing permission and confirmation contract. | Delete this draft? This cannot be undone. | Ready for a fresh start? |

Pricele's paper/night vocabulary belongs to its publication setting. Portfolio's bar and shelf belong to its owner. A generic client's navigation need not inherit either metaphor. Preserve proper names, acronyms and user-entered copy.
