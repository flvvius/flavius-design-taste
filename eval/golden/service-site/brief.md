# Service site brief

Audience: founders with an AI-built app or a small SaaS whose interface needs clearer hierarchy and better task flow.

Primary action: email Flavius with the app URL and one flow to review. The contact destination is the public address from portfolio data/site.ts. The page sends no email itself.

Message: Flavius audits and redesigns the interface directly in its codebase, delivering reviewable code and before/after evidence.

Constraints: one complete Next.js/Tailwind v4 page, reusable starter tokens, no client data, invented testimonials, metrics or new dependencies. All demonstration content is marked. No live deployment is included.

Content inventory: task-led headline, illustration of an input-boundary change, two offers supplied in the task, implementation sequence, public example, scope questions and a contact link. Prices and turnaround are draft service terms from this brief; confirm availability before publishing.

Page order: navigation, service promise and review margin, offer rows, working method, scope questions, contact footer.

Composition A: the headline leads on a wide reading column. A narrow reviewer margin states what to send and a code diff grounds the promise. Offers follow as two comparison rows.

Composition B: a large example patch leads on the left. The service question, intake checklist and action occupy the right, like an open review document. The content enters through evidence rather than a large headline.

Choice: A. A visitor can identify the service before interpreting an example patch. B is retained as a real alternative, with its own route and capture. The autonomous choice uses the user's approval to complete the work.

Direction: Editorial calm, with a restrained Georgia display role for the long headline, Inter for interface/prose and system monospace for the actual patch vocabulary. This is a site-specific type adaptation, not a third preset. Warm paper and graphite actions retain the existing semantic palette. One memorable subject idea is the reviewer margin and small code diff, because this service ships PRs.

Mobile: the margin follows the title, the patch becomes a readable horizontal-free block, and offer columns become vertical descriptions. The contact remains a normal link. Details use native controls.

Unknowns: final headline and terms, availability, domain, analytics and whether contact should move to a booking service. The implementation does not invent them. No form claims a message was sent.
